Research is only as good as your understanding of the underlying data. Here is what Venture Atlas measures, how it measures it, and where the evidence stops.

01 / SOURCE

A historical company snapshot

The app uses a cleaned copy of the project’s investments_VC.csv dataset. The bundled snapshot contains 49,438 company records; known founding years range from 1902 to 2014. These are founding years, not dates when the dataset was collected.

The repository does not include independently verified collection dates, complete provenance, or a source licence for the original CSV. Treat the snapshot as a portfolio research dataset, not an authoritative or current market source. It includes established businesses as well as startups.

Company names, market labels, country codes, and statuses are preserved from the supplied records. A status of “operating” describes the record at collection time.

02 / COVERAGE

Disclosed funding, not company value

“Company records” counts all matching records. “Total disclosed funding” sums available funding amounts in USD. The bundled dataset has 40,907 records with disclosed funding; the remaining records still appear in the directory.

Missing funding is shown as “Undisclosed” and excluded from funding averages and rankings. It is not replaced with zero. Total raised does not establish a valuation, a fundraising round, or a company’s present-day value.

Country and market counts include non-empty labels. A founding-year filter excludes records without a founding year. The dashboard’s filters apply to every analytics panel and the company directory; the research desk searches independently.

03 / TIME

A founding cohort is not a funding year

The cohort chart groups companies by their founding year, then sums their total disclosed lifetime funding. The cumulative view adds those cohort totals from the first included founding year onward. Years without an included company carry the running total forward.

Cumulative cohort funding(y) = sum of disclosed funding for companies founded on or before y

This cannot show annual investment flows, year-over-year fundraising velocity, or when a funding round occurred. The chart compares up to five leading markets in the selected population. Use “By cohort” to see each founding year separately.

04 / DISTRIBUTION

Following concentration at the top

We use SQL PERCENT_RANK() to rank the filtered records with positive disclosed funding. Records with a percentile rank of at least 0.99 are grouped by country and sorted by their total funding. Unknown countries stay in the ranking.

Ties may make this group larger or smaller than exactly one percent. Small populations or tied funding amounts can yield no records at that threshold. A country filter changes the population being ranked; it does not represent a comparison against the unfiltered world.

05 / INTERPRETATION

AI as a starting point for diligence

On request, the server sends the selected historical company profile to Groq’s hosted model. Your API key stays on the server. The model is instructed to describe a sector opportunity, a strength, and a risk using only the supplied fields, with uncertainty made explicit.

No live web search, current financial statements, valuation checks, or independent fact verification take place. A plausible sentence is not verified evidence. Review every note; it is not an investment recommendation.

Generated notes are cached on the server. Free hosting can reset that cache. “Save in this browser” keeps a local copy on this device; it does not create an account or sync across devices. New generation is limited to five requests per minute across the service, with additional provider quotas.

06 / IMPLEMENTATION

A small, inspectable research stack

Flask serves the pages and parameterized SQL APIs. The hosted app restores its bundled dataset into SQLite on startup; the original MySQL ETL and local Ollama workflow remain in the repository. Charts are drawn from API results with native SVG, without an external charting service.

Company search, pagination, exports, data definitions, request validation, and automated regression tests support the research flow. You can inspect the source and run the application locally.
